A community in early form

AI for Public Good

AI uses electricity, water, and land, and it is changing how we think and talk with each other. This is a place for people who want to do something about both: to offer time, skill, money, or introductions to projects that help communities, and to have that help noticed and returned.

We're early. We are still choosing how the community is run and funded, and nothing here is promised yet. If you want to shape it, tell us what you could offer.

Ways to help

You don't have to be an expert. Pick what you can give.

Time

Reviewing a draft, attending a hearing, a few calls each month.

Expertise

Water or energy engineering, data, law, design, writing, translation, field work.

Resources

Meeting space, equipment such as a sensor, software access, a dataset you can share.

Ideas

A project, a question, or a source we haven't covered.

Network

An introduction to a utility, council, researcher, funder, or community group.

Money

A gift to a named project. Money isn't accepted yet, while the structure is being set up.

Ideas already on the table

Each idea links to the public work it comes from. Some are early, and none has been tested yet.

Find leaks in city water pipes with AI

Water · seed

Water lost through leaking pipes is treated water that never reaches a customer. Sensor and meter data could rank the likely leaks for crews to check.

Cool data centers with reclaimed water, not drinking water

Water · seed

Facilities can use treated wastewater for cooling instead of water people drink, and publish how much they use.

Community-run air sensors with open, checked data

Air · seed

Neighborhoods with few air monitors could host their own sensors, calibrated against nearby official ones.

How it works

  1. Offer. Choose a project or the community as a whole, and say what you can give. Your name is credited the way you choose.
  2. We connect you. Someone reaches out to talk about what would help and what you'd like in return. Contact details are shared only if you agree.
  3. Help is confirmed. The project lead confirms when help is given, and it's recorded. Projects with offers from several people are marked as needing hands.

There are no leaderboards. Recognition is for help that was actually given, and nobody is ranked against anyone else.

What we are not doing yet

  • Accepting money. The structure has to be set up first, and the first gifts will come with a public report on what they did.
  • Publishing badges. Recognition is kept private for now.
  • Claiming results. The ideas listed here are proposals, and their figures come from public summaries we are still checking.
