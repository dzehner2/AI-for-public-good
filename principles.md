# Design Principles

Every idea card should be able to answer yes to these questions. A "no" is allowed,
but it must be written down and given a mitigation.

1. **Net-positive footprint.** After counting the AI system's own electricity, cooling
   water, hardware, and land, does the idea still improve the outcome? Show the arithmetic.
2. **Water-aware siting.** Does the idea avoid drawing on water that a stressed watershed
   or a vulnerable community needs? Prefer reclaimed, captured, or non-potable sources.
3. **Clean-power alignment.** Does the compute or sensing run when and where clean power
   is available, or does it cost the grid something at peak?
4. **Data sovereignty.** Who owns the data, who can revoke it, and who gets the benefit?
   Community-generated data stays under community governance by default.
5. **Human accountability.** AI produces signals, forecasts, or drafts. A named, accountable
   person or body makes the decision and can override the system.
6. **Open enough to audit.** Models, data definitions, and performance metrics can be
   inspected by regulators, researchers, and affected residents.
7. **Reversible pilots.** Every pilot has a small scope, a success metric, and a stop rule
   agreed before it starts.
8. **Serve the least-resourced first.** Name the community that benefits most and check
   that it is not the one that bears the costs.
9. **Works without the cloud where it must.** Critical alerts and information must still
   reach people during outages. Edge or offline fallbacks are required for safety-critical uses.
10. **Plain-language outputs.** Results are published in language residents can act on,
    with uncertainty stated.
