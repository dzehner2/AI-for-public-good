# Ways to Help

You can offer time, expertise, resources, ideas, network connections, or money to any project in the
library, or to the community as a whole. You don't need to be an expert, and no offer is too small.

## Pick what you can give

| Offer | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Time** | Reviewing a draft, attending a hearing, phone calls, a few hours a month |
| **Expertise** | Water or energy engineering, data analysis, law, design, writing, translation, facilitation, field work |
| **Resources** | Meeting space, equipment such as a sensor, software access, a dataset you're allowed to share |
| **Ideas** | A project, a question, or a source we haven't covered |
| **Network** | An introduction to a utility, council, researcher, funder, or community group |
| **Money** | A gift to a named project. Money is accepted only after the community's stewardship step is in place, and every gift gets a public report on what it did |

## How to offer

1. **Choose a project.** Browse the ideas in the library (each card lists where you could help), or
   use `general` for the community as a whole.
2. **Send the offer.** Reply to the post or message that brought you here, or use this template:

   ```
   Project: [idea name, or "general"]
   Offer type: [time / expertise / resources / ideas / network / money]
   What I can give:
   When I'm available:
   Name to credit (or "anonymous"):
   May we contact you about this? [yes / no]
   Contact (only if you said yes):
   ```

3. **Expect a reply.** Someone will confirm what they received and suggest a next step, within
   two weeks.

## What happens to your offer

- Your offer is recorded with the name you chose. Contact details are kept only if you said yes,
  and they are never published.
- You can change or withdraw your offer at any time by writing to the same address.
- Nothing you offer is used as data, quoted, or published without your agreement.

## What we ask in return

- **Be honest about your time.** It is better to offer one evening a month and keep it than to promise more.
- **Respect the knowledge you're offered.** Indigenous and local knowledge shared with us is not ours to
  reuse without agreement.
- **Disagree in the open.** A critical review is as valuable as an endorsement.
