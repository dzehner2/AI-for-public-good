# Landscape: Existing Work to Connect To

Purpose: show members and invitees what is already underway, so the community joins existing
efforts rather than duplicating them. Compiled from web search results (June 2026). Summaries are
from search snippets; check each source before citing it in a proposal.

## 1. Data center community benefit agreements and trust

- **Trust as a constraint.** Industry commentary argues community trust now limits projects as much as
  power and water do, and calls for standard engagement frameworks.
  ([Data Center Frontier](https://www.datacenterfrontier.com/site-selection/podcast/55397375/the-next-data-center-constraint-trust))
- **Secret negotiations.** The Foundation for American Security reports that developer and municipal
  talks often run under NDAs, and located eight executed agreements by searching for them.
  ([FAS](https://fas.org/?p=47528))
- **Model framework.** WRI describes a Detroit-style Community Benefits Ordinance as a law-based
  template that project agreements can build on, with water-use caps among its example terms.
  ([WRI](https://www.wri.org/technical-perspectives/community-benefits-agreements-data-centers))
- **Water terms.** Brookings suggests agreements cover water use, electric rates, noise, and public
  dashboards. ([Tennessee Lookout, citing Brookings](https://tennesseelookout.com/2026/06/26/heres-what-communities-can-do-when-data-centers-arrive/))

**Connection point:** the community's benefit-sharing and data-trust ideas sit directly on this work.
Members with local government or community-organizing ties are the bridge.

## 2. Water and energy transparency

- **State action.** New Jersey requires data centers to report water and energy use to state regulators
  every six months, and more than a dozen states were considering similar laws in 2026.
  ([GoodGoodGood](https://www.goodgoodgood.co/articles/new-jersey-data-center-law-report-water-and-energy-use),
  [NewsFromTheStates](https://www.newsfromthestates.com/node/424756))
- **Federal bill.** The Data Center Water and Energy Transparency Act was introduced in March 2026 and would
  make the data available to local governments. Status not checked.
  ([NGWA](https://www.ngwa.org/detail/news/2026/03/30/senator-introduces-legislation-to-bring-transparency-to-water-consumption-by-data-centers))
- **Voluntary disclosure.** Microsoft's 2026 sustainability report includes site-level water and power data.
  ([Data Center Richness](https://datacenterrichness.substack.com/p/microsoft-shares-data-center-campus))
- **Community tools.** The Great Lakes Commission publishes a playbook with checklists for residents.
  ([greatlakes.org](https://greatlakes.org/?p=24470))

**Caution:** national water estimates conflict. One figure is about 17 billion gallons for 2023, and a
GMFUS analysis gives about 228 billion. Check methodology before citing either.
([GMFUS](https://www.gmfus.org/news/water-and-ai-infrastructure-boom))

## 3. Demand flexibility and carbon-aware compute

- **Google's platform.** Google shifts workloads toward cleaner grid conditions, and now runs demand
  response with grid operators, limiting non-urgent tasks during forecast supply shortfalls. Reported
  power reduction in one deployment: 1–2%.
  ([Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/infrastructure/using-demand-response-to-reduce-data-center-power-consumption),
  [Utility Dive](https://utilitydive.com/news/google-carbon-intelligent-computing-platform-system-reliability-demand-response-grid-emergency/698958))
- **Research.** Academic work proposes using both time and location flexibility of compute jobs, with
  simulated reductions in carbon and peak power. Simulation only.
  ([arXiv 2410.21510](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.21510v2))

**Connection point:** the grid-aware scheduling idea. The community's energy engineer seat fits here.

## 4. Cognitive offloading and critical thinking

- **Main study.** Gerlich (2025), in *Societies*, surveyed 666 participants and found an association
  between heavier AI use and weaker critical thinking, with cognitive offloading as the likely mechanism.
  Younger users and less-educated users scored lower. This is correlational, not proof of causation.
  ([PsyPost](https://www.psypost.org/ai-tools-may-weaken-critical-thinking-skills-by-encouraging-cognitive-offloading-study-suggests),
  [ZME Science](https://zmescience.com/science/news-science/ai-hurting-our-critical-thinking-skills))
- **Dissent.** Critics note that offloading to tools has long been normal, and the real question is which
  reasoning steps people give up. The search found no independent replication.

**Gap:** the community's cognitive-health workstream needs experimental work on AI-assisted judgment and
on tools that return sources and questions instead of answers. This search did not cover that literature.

## 5. Indigenous data sovereignty

- **CARE principles.** Collective benefit, authority to control, responsibility, and ethics, drafted
  through the Global Indigenous Data Alliance.
  ([CASRAI](https://casrai.org/learn/what-are-the-care-principles))
- **AI safeguards.** An exploratory paper argues for Local Contexts Labels (Traditional Knowledge and
  Biocultural labels) in generative AI development.
  ([Recal](https://recal.rcaap.pt/recal/article/download/65/45/337))
- **Caution.** The Native Nations Institute warns that researchers may cite the CARE principles in place of
  asking communities directly. ([Native Nations Institute](https://www.nni.arizona.edu/news/acta-borealia-care-principles-ten-years-in))

**Connection point:** the knowledge-partner terms in `team-and-priorities.md`. Most results here are
secondary, so read the original CARE and Local Contexts materials directly.

## 6. Civic technology and ethics networks

- **Cities for Digital Rights.** An AI & Data Community of Practice of member cities.
  ([citiesfordigitalrights.org](https://citiesfordigitalrights.org/node/273))
- **Co-operative Councils' Innovation Network (UK).** Councils, civil society, and technologists working on
  democratic procurement. ([P2P Foundation](https://wiki.p2pfoundation.net/Cooperative_Values-Driven_AI))
- **Quinnipiac ETHOS.** A reciprocal model: students learn from community ethical challenges, and
  residents contribute professional perspectives. ([Quinnipiac](https://www.qu.edu/info-for/ethical-technology-use-through-humanities-outreach-and-shared-learning/))
- **JUST AI network.** A research-practitioner network at LSE with the Ada Lovelace Institute.
  ([Ada Lovelace Institute](https://adalovelaceinstitute.org/?p=10419))
- **Civic Tech DC.** Validates projects with communities that hold local trust.
  ([Technical.ly](https://technical.ly/civics/civic-tech-dc-rebuilds-trust-at-scale/))

**Gap:** no dedicated reciprocity network for local AI was found. The Quinnipiac model is the closest
match and is worth a direct conversation.

## Unverified or unresolved

- Data center water totals (see section 2) need a methodology check.
- The Michigan community benefits bill was reported in a search result, but its current status was not checked.
- Gerlich (2025) is a single correlational study with no replication found.
- CARE and Local Contexts material is mostly secondary. Read originals before citing.

## How to use this in invitations

Name one or two existing efforts that match a person's offer, rather than listing everything. For
example: a utility or council contact can be pointed to the agreement and transparency work in
section 1 and 2; a researcher can be pointed to the cognitive-offloading question in section 4.
